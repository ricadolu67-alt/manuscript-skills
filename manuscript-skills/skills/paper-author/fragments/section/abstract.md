# Abstract

Write the abstract last, from the talk note. Every sentence has one job; choose the jobs you need, in the order the talk note tells the story.

| Job | What it says | From the ledger |
|---|---|---|
| Question | The object of study and one concrete question | P1, P2 |
| Surprise | The most concrete counter-intuitive fact, with its number | `surprise`, `plain_reading`, `story_role: setup` |
| Main result | The key quantity: rate, threshold, exponent, measured value | `statement` of the DECISIVE claims |
| Mechanism | Why it holds, in a reader's words | `mechanism` |
| What changes | An improvement, a separation, or a new understanding relative to prior work | P4 |
| Further results | One sentence for extensions or examples | payoff, SUPPORTING, EXTENSION |

## Conditions

A claim in the abstract carries its `reader_condition` and nothing else. Technical hypotheses, constants, truncations and offsets stay in the theorems. A sentence that would need two chained conditions usually reads better as "in the regime defined below".

## The last sentence

End on what changes, or on the most consequential implication. Avoid a vague summary ("These results highlight the role of …"), and do not end on a caveat.

## Check

Name each sentence's job; delete any sentence that has none. Compare the order of the jobs with the talk note. Tier C of [native_criteria.md](../../references/native_criteria.md) gives the usual shape and the usual limits on symbols, coined terms and condition clauses. Treat them as defaults.
