# Optional writing exemplars

Exemplars teach register: how a paper introduces its question, states a quantitative answer, explains the key idea and compares with earlier work. They supply no scientific content for a new manuscript.

## Choosing and using an exemplar

Use one or two papers the user can lawfully access and that match the target venue and paper type. Read the abstract, the end of the introduction, a main result and its explanation, and the discussion where relevant. Record the passages actually read; do not claim access to unavailable text.

Look for how each paragraph completes an inference, how terms are introduced, and how hypotheses are placed next to formal results. Learn those decisions without copying sentences, distinctive phrases, formulas, figures or sentence skeletons.

Do not import results or citations into a new manuscript merely because they occur in an exemplar. Scientific assertions and prior-work comparisons must be supported by the project's public ledger.

## When no exemplar is available

Proceed with [native_criteria.md](../references/native_criteria.md) and [defensive_vs_native.md](../references/defensive_vs_native.md). Record `exemplars unavailable` in the author notes. Do not block drafting, invent a reading history or imitate a remembered paper.

No third-party paper copies are distributed with this project. Acquiring or redistributing full text requires the applicable access and reuse permissions. Public availability alone is not redistribution permission.
